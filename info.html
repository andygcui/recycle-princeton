Learn About Recycling

Why Recycling Matters

Recycling helps protect our planet by reducing waste and saving resources! When we recycle correctly, we:

  • Save energy and natural resources
  • Reduce pollution
  • Keep our communities clean
  • Help create new products from old materials
Contamination is the Worst!

Contamination happens when wrong items go into recycling bins. This is a huge problem because:

  • One wrong item can ruin an entire batch of recyclables
  • Contaminated recycling often ends up in landfills instead
  • It costs money and time to sort out bad items
  • It can damage recycling equipment

That's why it's so important to learn which plastics go where! When you sort correctly, you're helping make recycling work for everyone.

Understanding Plastic Codes in Your Area!

Every plastic item has a number inside a triangle of arrows. This number tells you what type of plastic it is!

#1
PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate)
Widely recyclable in Princeton! This is one of the most common recyclable plastics.
Examples: Water bottles, soda bottles, food containers
#2
HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene)
Widely recyclable in Princeton! Very common and easy to recycle.
Examples: Milk jugs, detergent bottles, shampoo bottles
#3
PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)
NOT recyclable curbside in Princeton. These items should go in the trash.
Examples: PVC pipes, vinyl flooring, some food wraps
#4
LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene)
Requires special dropoff in Princeton. Don't put these in curbside recycling!
Examples: Plastic bags, shrink wrap, some food wraps
#5
PP (Polypropylene)
Requires special dropoff in Princeton. Check with your local recycling center.
Examples: Yogurt cups, bottle caps, takeout containers
#6
PS (Polystyrene)
NOT recyclable curbside in Princeton. These should go in the trash.
Examples: Styrofoam cups, disposable plates, packing peanuts
#7
Other/Mixed Plastics
NOT recyclable curbside in Princeton. These are hard to recycle and should go in the trash.
Examples: Mixed plastics, bioplastics, some food containers

Tips for Better Recycling

  • Rinse containers clean - Food residue can contaminate recycling
  • Check the number - Look for the recycling symbol with the number
  • When in doubt, throw it out - It's better to put something in trash than contaminate recycling
  • Remove caps - Check if your area wants caps on or off (Princeton: usually off)
  • No plastic bags in curbside - These need special dropoff
  • Keep it dry - Wet paper and cardboard can't be recycled
